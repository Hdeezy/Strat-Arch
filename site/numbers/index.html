Context and evidence

What the homepage numbers mean.

2,129

People recorded as experiencing homelessness in Hamilton

Source
City of Hamilton Housing and Homelessness Dashboard and its authoritative June data.
When
June 30, 2026.
Population
People recorded in Hamilton's Homeless Individuals and Families Information System (HIFIS), including people with a shelter stay in the previous 90 days.
Basis
Administrative homelessness-system data. It is not a one-night census of every person experiencing homelessness.
Limit
The count changes as people enter or leave homelessness and as records are updated. It does not measure HOPE.

48 days

Sooner into stable housing, New Leaf Project (British Columbia)

Source
Foundations for Social Change, New Leaf Project impact summary, with the University of British Columbia. Peer-reviewed as "Unconditional cash transfers reduce homelessness," Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (2023).
When
Pilot in Metro Vancouver; results published 2020 (preliminary) and 2023 (peer-reviewed).
Population
115 people who had recently become homeless were randomly assigned to cash and non-cash groups. 50 cash recipients each received a one-time, unconditional $7,500. Eligibility screening excluded severe substance use and major mental-health symptoms.
Basis
Cash recipients moved into stable housing in 96 days on average, against 144 days for the comparison group: a difference of 48 days.
Limit
A small, screened sample in one region. HOPE will not give cash transfers; it will pay vendors and providers directly. The result is context, not a forecast of HOPE's impact.

$8,100

Shelter costs saved per person in a year, New Leaf Project

Source
Foundations for Social Change, New Leaf Project impact summary.
When
First year after the transfer.
Population
The 50 cash recipients described above.
Basis
Fewer nights in shelter saved the shelter system about $8,100 per person, roughly $405,000 across 50 recipients over one year. The project reports a net saving of about $600 per person after the $7,500 transfer.
Limit
Reflects Vancouver shelter costs and counts shelter costs only. Hamilton's cost per shelter stay will replace it in HOPE's own reporting once confirmed.

86%

Still out of homelessness 6 to 9 months later, Greater Change (United Kingdom)

Source
Greater Change, Test and Learn: Evaluating the Effectiveness of Personalised Budgets (July 30, 2025), citing its own impact report. Program overview at greaterchange.co.uk.
When
People supported in the year before that report.
Population
People supported with personalised budgets agreed with a support worker and paid to providers. Greater Change reports about 2,500 people supported to date.
Basis
"86% of those supported last year maintaining their move out of homelessness between 6 and 9 months later."
Limit
This is the program's own follow-up figure, not a controlled trial result. A randomised trial of 390 participants, funded by the UK government and delivered with the Centre for Homelessness Impact, is expected to report in fall 2026.
Illustrative · not a forecast

$810K

A year in shelter costs freed if 100 Hamiltonians reach a stable restoration

Method
100 × $8,100, the New Leaf per-person shelter saving above.
The dial
The homepage dial applies the same arithmetic to any number from 10 to 500. It also shows a comparison cost of n × $7,500, New Leaf's one-time transfer, and the share of Hamilton's 2,129 recorded count.
What it is not
A forecast, a target or a measured HOPE result. It does not use Hamilton costs or HOPE's cost per restoration, which the pilot will measure and publish.
Success measure
HOPE will count a stable restoration only if it still holds six months on.

Further context

Background figures.

$191.6M

Hamilton's 2025 housing and homelessness investment

Source
City of Hamilton, Whole of Hamilton Housing Approach.
When
2025 municipal budget.
Population
City-wide housing and homelessness programs and investments, not a per-person amount.
Basis
$191.6 million in total planned investment, including $130.2 million supported by the municipal tax levy.
Limit
This is a public-budget context figure. It is not HOPE funding, savings or projected revenue.

69%

Cost offset reported for high-need Housing First participants

Source
Cost-Effectiveness of Housing First With Assertive Community Treatment, Psychiatric Services (2020).
When
Paper published in 2020; trial activity ran from 2009 to 2013.
Population
Adults with serious mental illness and high support needs who received Housing First with Assertive Community Treatment across five Canadian sites.
Basis
The study reported that reductions in other service use offset about 69% of intervention costs. Its reported annual averages were $20,367 gross and about $6,311 net per person after offsets.
Limit
HOPE is not the intervention studied. The population, clinical intensity, period and cost structure differ, so the result is context, not a forecast or proof of HOPE's impact.

Method

How this page will change.

Figures are reviewed against their linked sources when the website is materially updated. A number will be corrected or retired if its source changes, its definition is no longer clear or a newer directly comparable figure becomes available.

Future HOPE reporting will separate project activity, member outcomes, donor information and outside research. Small-group results will be withheld or combined when publishing them could identify a member.
