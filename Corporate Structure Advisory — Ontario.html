IP isolation & tax shield

SR&ED
43% R&D subsidy (CCPC rate)
Federal 35% fully refundable on first $3M of qualifying expenditures, plus Ontario OITC 8%. For every $100 of AI dev spend, roughly $43 returns as cash before deducting the expense.
IP Freeze
Transfer at cost while IP is young
Use ITA s.85 rollover to transfer IP to TechCo at nominal value. All future appreciation — the entire commercial value of the AI platform — is trapped in TechCo shares at LCGE-sheltered capital gains rates.
Royalties
Deductible by payer, taxed at 9%
BenefitsCo and MGA deduct royalties as a business expense. TechCo receives them at the CCPC small business rate (9% federal on first $500K). Document arm's-length rate with an IP valuation.
QSBC test
TechCo shares = LCGE eligible
Ensure TechCo stays CCPC-qualified: 90%+ of FMV in active Canadian business assets, 24-month holding period, less than $50M taxable capital. Each trust beneficiary claims their own $1.25M+ exemption on exit.

Data moat
The trust as competitive shield
Brokers contribute anonymized data to the trust in exchange for membership benefits. The trust — not TechCo — owns the aggregated dataset. TechCo is merely a licensee. PIPEDA-defensible and impossible for any competitor to replicate by throwing money at it.
NFP tax
ITA s.149(1)(e) exemption
The association qualifies as a non-profit under CRA guidance — member dues and community activities are exempt from Part I tax. Commercial revenue flows to the wholly-owned subsidiary only, keeping NFP status clean and audit-proof.
Flywheel
More members = better AI
Every new brokerage member adds data to the trust, improving model accuracy. Better models attract more brokerages. Better benchmarking data improves proposal quality. The community can't be unbundled — it is the product.
CRA risk
NFP commercial benefit scrutiny
CRA scrutinizes NFPs that generate commercial benefit for related parties. Board independence, genuine member value, and substantive community purpose are mandatory. A sales funnel wearing a charity logo fails the s.149 test.
Framework 4 of 6

Family wealth architecture

LCGE multiplication, prescribed rate loans, estate freeze, and three integration paths for a related brokerage — coordinated into a single tax-efficient structure.

LCGE
Multiply the lifetime exemption
Each trust beneficiary can claim their own ~$1.25M LCGE on qualifying TechCo shares at exit. A family of 4 = up to $5M of capital gains completely tax-free. Structure from day one — retrofitting is complicated.
PRL
Prescribed rate loan income split
HoldCo loans funds to the family trust at the CRA prescribed rate. Trust invests; income earned above the prescribed rate is taxed in lower-income beneficiaries' hands. Annual interest must be paid by January 30 or attribution rules kick in.
Dad — A
Estate freeze + succession
Dad exchanges brokerage common shares for fixed-value preferred shares via ITA s.85/86 reorganization. New common shares (with all future growth) go to the family trust. Future appreciation passes to next generation tax-deferred.
Dad — B
Charter association member
Dad's brokerage becomes a founding member of the NFP Association with preferred AI tool pricing, early access, and a revenue share on referred brokerages. Zero structural complexity — maximum optionality preserved.
Dad — C
Book-of-business acquisition
BenefitsCo acquires Dad's client list and AUM at FMV. Dad receives a capital gain sheltered by LCGE if his shares qualify as QSBC. BenefitsCo gets the clients immediately, accelerating active business income.
EOT (2023+)
Employee Ownership Trust
A qualifying EOT acquires TechCo shares — employees become economic owners without cash. Sellers receive a separate $10M capital gains exemption (additive to LCGE). Powerful talent retention tool few Canadian operators know exists yet.
Framework 5 of 6

HoldCo creditor firewall

How the corporate veil works, what creditors can and cannot reach, the three attack vectors, and the timing discipline required to keep the structure defensible.

Click any entity node for a detailed advisory note

How it works
Judgment stops at the OpCo
A judgment against BenefitsCo is against BenefitsCo only. Courts will not pierce the corporate veil to reach HoldCo assets unless a creditor can satisfy one of the three attack vectors — all defeated by consistent, early, well-documented transfers.
Attack vector 1
Fraudulent preference (BIA)
A transfer to HoldCo within one year of insolvency (five years at undervalue) can be unwound. Defense: transfers must be at FMV, made well before any insolvency is foreseeable, and consistent with normal cash management practice — not reactive to a known claim.
Attack vector 2
GAAR (ITA s.245)
CRA's tool for challenging arrangements that technically comply but produce unintended results. Defense: genuine business purpose, arm's-length pricing, documented contemporaneously, consistent year-over-year — not a spike triggered by adverse events.
Attack vector 3
Oppression remedy (OBCA s.248)
Broadest attack vector — doesn't require fraud, only unfairly prejudicial conduct. Courts have used this where a shareholder drained OpCo assets into HoldCo after a dispute arose. Defense: consistent commercially rational behavior, not reactive to specific claims.
Framework 6 of 6

Grey legal arsenal

Aggressive-but-legal strategies ranked by risk level. All permissible under Canadian law — several require qualified tax counsel to execute correctly.

Standard
ITA s.112 inter-corporate dividends
Dividends from a subsidiary CCPC to a parent CCPC are 100% deductible — effectively zero tax on the transfer. The non-negotiable foundation of every Canadian holding company structure. Set this up before any profit is generated.
Standard
SR&ED + Ontario OITC
Up to 43% of qualifying R&D costs returned as cash. Well-established but heavily audited — document everything contemporaneously. Technological uncertainty must be genuine, and project logs must be written in real time, not reconstructed.
Standard
LCGE multiplication
Multiple family trust beneficiaries each claim their $1.25M+ LCGE on qualifying QSBC shares. Legal and well-understood — requires correct setup from day one. The 24-month holding test, 90% active asset test, and taxable capital limit must all be satisfied continuously.
Aggressive
IP freeze via s.85 rollover
Transfer IP to TechCo at nominal value while the company is young. All future appreciation sits in TechCo at capital gains / LCGE rates. CRA scrutinizes if the elected amount is unreasonably low — commission a proper IP valuation and document it.
Aggressive
Management fee stripping
HoldCo charges OpCos for services at FMV — legally strips income into the passive holding entity. Must be backed by a real intercompany services agreement and actual services rendered. Circular fees with no substance are a s.245 GAAR target.
Aggressive
NFP association as lead feeder
CRA scrutinizes NFPs that commercially benefit related parties. Genuine member value, an independent board, and substantive community purpose are mandatory. The association must pass the "operated exclusively for non-profit purposes" test — not just on paper.
Aggressive
Data cooperative / trust
Genuinely novel — no specific CRA guidance and no settled caselaw. Sound under Ontario contract and trust law, and PIPEDA-defensible as a community-owned data asset. First-mover advantage on the moat. Requires tech-IP lawyer and tax counsel working together.
Aggressive
DataCo infrastructure leaseback
DataCo owns servers, networking, and cloud hardware — leased back to TechCo and OpCos at FMV. DataCo claims Class 8 (20%), Class 10 (30%), and Class 53 (100%) CCA accelerated deductions, stripping rent expense from TechCo.
Handle carefully
Pipeline transaction (on exit)
Converts retained earnings from dividend treatment to capital gains on corporate sale. CRA has successfully challenged these (Foix, Descarries). Still used — but only with current safe-harbor planning, documented economic substance, and explicit legal opinion.
Handle carefully
International IP structure
IP holding company in UK or Ireland (treaty access + lower rates), licensed down to the Canadian OpCo. Canadian FAPI rules limit benefit unless there's genuine foreign substance — real employees, real premises, real functions. A pure shell is an immediate CRA attack.
Implementation note: This is a strategic framework, not legal or tax advice. A Canadian corporate tax lawyer (specializing in reorganizations) and a CPA firm with private-company M&A experience should review and implement the final structure. The combination of LCGE multiplication + SR&ED + Data Trust + NFP association is genuinely novel — budget $15–30K for initial legal and accounting setup; the LCGE savings alone on exit justify multiples of that.
